Designing a C++ library four teams wanted to use

March 2026 9 min C++

The hard part of an internal library isn't the code. It's that four teams have to agree the thing is worth depending on, and none of them owe you that.

This is a scaffold — the structure is real, the words are placeholder. It shows every piece of the writing system so you can see how a post behaves at full length.

Body text sits at 16.5px on a 640px measure, which lands around seventy characters a line. Margin notes sit out to the right on wide screens and drop inline below 1120px, anchored to the paragraph they belong to.

This is a margin note. Good for an aside that would break the sentence if you inlined it — a definition, a caveat, a bit of context.

Callouts are a rule and a label

No fill, no icon, no coloured box. A 2px accent rule and a small mono label is enough to mark something as set apart, and it stays quiet on the page.

Worth knowing If you need more than one kind of callout, you probably need better paragraphs. One kind keeps the page calm.

Figures

Captions stay short. If a figure needs a paragraph to explain it, the paragraph belongs in the body.
